# Facebook Reels: recommended daily posting volume for a GTA 6 "cortes" page (PT-BR, gaming niche)

Research date: 2026-11-15. Source types are labeled throughout: **[OFFICIAL]** = Meta; **[DATA]** = third-party data study; **[VENDOR/BLOG]** = tool vendor or marketing blog opinion; **[ANECDOTE]** = single-account report. Older (pre-2024) sources are flagged **(OLDER)**.

## 1. What Meta officially says about posting frequency for Pages/creators

### Takeaway
Meta publishes **no number** for Reels per day or per week. Its guidance is qualitative ("post high-quality content frequently and consistently"). The numbers it does publish are about penalties: from 2025 on, it cuts reach and monetization for spam, "flooding Feed" and unoriginal reposts. So the only hard number from Meta is the API ceiling (see section 4).

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] **(OLDER, Oct 5, 2022)** Meta's creator best-practices page says "Posting high-quality content frequently can increase opportunities for audiences" to see content they like. It also says "Posting consistently gives viewers another reason to return often" and "one size doesn't fit all." It gives no posts/day or posts/week target. It still describes Reels as "up to 60 seconds", which is out of date (see the API doc below). — [Meta for Creators: Best practices for growing your Facebook presence](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/best-practices-for-growing-your-facebook-presence)
- [OFFICIAL] **Apr 24, 2025 spam crackdown.** "Accounts gaming distribution and engagement or flooding Feed with spammy content will see less views and monetization." Networks of accounts sharing the same content "will not be eligible for monetization and may see lower audience reach." Accounts using long or unrelated captions with excessive hashtags "will only have their content shown to their followers." The post sets **no numeric posting-volume limit**. — [Meta Newsroom: Cracking Down on Spammy Content on Facebook](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/cracking-down-spammy-content-facebook/); also [Meta for Creators blog version](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/cracking-down-on-spammy-content); coverage: [TechCrunch, Apr 24 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/24/facebook-cracks-down-on-spammy-content-by-cutting-reach-and-monetization)
- [OFFICIAL, via press] **Jul 14, 2025 "Combating unoriginal content".** Meta targets accounts that repeatedly repost other creators' content "without permission or meaningful enhancements". Repeat offenders lose monetization for a period and get reduced distribution of their posts. When duplicates are detected, the copies get less distribution so the original creator keeps the views. Content where creators "add their unique take" (commentary, reactions) is said not to be the target. In H1 2025 Meta acted on about 500,000 accounts for spammy behavior or fake engagement. It also added post-level insights in the Professional Dashboard and a way to see whether a Page is at risk of distribution or monetization penalties. — [Meta for Creators: Combating unoriginal content](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content/?locale=en_GB); [TechCrunch, Jul 14 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [Tubefilter, Jul 15 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/)
- [PRESS, about Meta] An eMarketer piece is titled "Meta's latest creator updates encourage original content and consistent posting". That suggests Meta stressed "consistent posting" in a 2025 creator update, but the page returned 403 and the content could not be checked. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-s-latest-creator-updates-encourage-original-content-consistent-posting)
- [OFFICIAL] The Reels Publishing API doc says Reels can be published **only to Facebook Pages, not personal profiles**, and that standard reels run **3 to 90 seconds** (60 s max when published as a Page story). The user's 15–90 s clips fit. — [Meta for Developers: Reels Publishing API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/video-api/guides/reels-publishing)

### Inferences
- Meta's message is "frequent and consistent, but not spammy, not duplicated, not unoriginal." The penalty is triggered by **behavior and originality signals, not a published count**. For this project the bigger risk is probably the type of content (edited clips of other people's GTA 6 videos) and posting the same clip to several Pages, more than the number of posts per day.
- The Page-at-risk / monetization-status view in the Professional Dashboard is the official early-warning tool. The tool should prompt the user to check it after raising volume.

### Gaps
- No official Meta number (2024–2026) for ideal or maximum Reels per day per Page could be found.
- Facebook Content Monetization: no publicly documented **posting-volume requirement** (such as "X reels per month") was found. Current eligibility thresholds were not verified.
- The full text of Meta's 2025 "consistent posting" creator update (the eMarketer source) could not be read (403).

## 2. What third-party data studies say (posts/day vs. reach/engagement)

### Takeaway
Brand-page data puts typical Facebook volume at roughly **1–2 posts/day across all formats**, with **Reels at about 7 per month (~1–2 per week)** for the average brand. Reels also have the highest engagement rate per post. **No 2025–2026 study isolates "Reels/day vs. reach" for creator or clip pages**, and none measures the 5–20 Reels/day range this project can produce.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] **Socialinsider posting-frequency study** (published May 26 2026, updated Nov 11 2026). Sample: **24.6M organic Facebook posts**, Oct 2025–Mar 2026, from brand Pages with 1K–1M followers. Average monthly posts and engagement rate by format: **Reels 7/month, 0.20% ER (highest)**; Albums 6, 0.17%; Images 10, 0.15%; Statuses 6, 0.13%; Links 10, 0.05%. Its conclusion: the most-posted formats underperform, and Reels deliver more with less volume. Its advice is "consistency over volume", with **no specific Reels/week number**. — [Socialinsider: Social Media Posting Frequency vs Engagement](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-posting-frequency/)
- [DATA] Socialinsider's 2026 Facebook benchmarks say brands cut posting volume by **22% YoY to an average of 39 posts/month** (~9/week, all formats). This is from a search-result summary, not a full read. — [Socialinsider 2026 Facebook Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook). A UK agency citing Socialinsider instead says posting fell **48%, from 47 to 24 posts/month, 2024→2025**. The two figures conflict, probably because they come from different editions or samples. — [Giraffe Social Media](https://www.giraffesocialmedia.co.uk/?p=17355)
- [DATA/VENDOR] **Buffer frequency guide (Jan 13, 2026):** "You should post on Facebook once a day", with a quick reference of **1–2 posts/day** for a Page. The number comes from an older **HubSpot study of 13,500+ Facebook users**, not from Buffer's own data. The guide gives **no Facebook Reels figure** and does not discuss diminishing returns for Facebook. It also cites a cross-platform Buffer analysis of 100,000+ users in which consistent posters got "5x more engagement" (undated, not Facebook-specific). — [Buffer: How Often to Post on Social Media in 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/social-media-frequency-guide/)
- [DATA] Buffer's 2026 benchmarks give a **median of 35 Facebook posts/month** and a median engagement rate of 3.6%. This is from a search-result summary; the methodology was not read. — [Buffer 2026 Social Media Benchmarks](https://www.buffer.com/resources/social-media-benchmarks/)
- [DATA] Buffer's best-time study (Mar 2025, 1M+ posts) covers **timing, not frequency**. It found 5 a.m. Monday to be the top slot. — [Buffer: Best Time to Post on Facebook](https://buffer.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook)
- [VENDOR/BLOG, secondary] Secondary summaries attribute **~1–2 posts/day** to Hootsuite and **~3–4 valuable posts/week** for small businesses to Sprout Social. The original Hootsuite and Sprout pages were not reached, so these attributions are unverified. — e.g., [Social Champ: How often should you post on Facebook](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-facebook/)
- [VENDOR/BLOG] Reels-specific opinions from marketing and tool blogs vary widely and give no data:
  - "post at least 2 to 3 Facebook Reels a day" — [Social Champ](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/how-to-post-reels-on-facebook/)
  - 1–2 Reels/day "for niches Facebook promotes", and 3–5 Reels/week as the "fastest path" to monetization eligibility — 2026 guides at [fluxnote.io](https://fluxnote.io/guides/facebook-reels-monetization-requirements-2026) / [biztoolkit.co](https://www.biztoolkit.co/post/how-to-make-money-on-facebook-as-a-creator-in-2026) (attribution between the two is uncertain; seen only in search summaries)
  - 3–4 per week "for maximum discovery" — [Zozimus Agency](https://zozimus.com/facebook-best-practices/)
  - A PT-BR source: no fixed number; post what you can sustain without losing quality, which for most people means a few per week (Instagram-leaning) — [Kontentino PT](https://www.kontentino.com/pt/perguntas-e-respostas/quantos-reels-por-dia/)

### Inferences
- All of the data studies measure **brand Pages** that post a mix of formats. Their averages (1–2 posts/day overall, ~2 Reels/week) describe what brands *do*, not a ceiling for creator or clip Pages. No study shows reach per Reel falling at 3, 5 or 10 Reels/day. That threshold is simply unmeasured in public data.
- Because Reels carry the highest engagement per post on Facebook, a Reels-only clip Page has more room to post frequently than a brand Page posting links and images. Still, no study quantifies that room.
- The evidence-supported range for a "safe, consistent" baseline is about **1–3 Reels/day**. Anything above that is driven by anecdote and has to be tested per Page.

### Gaps
- No Metricool, Emplifi or Rival IQ study on Facebook Reels frequency vs. reach (2025–2026) was found.
- No study segments creator, meme or clip Pages from brand Pages, and none covers the gaming niche or Brazil specifically.
- Hootsuite and Sprout Social original 2025–2026 Facebook frequency pages were not accessed.

## 3. What creators say in forums and blogs (clip/meme pages, overposting)

### Takeaway
Reddit could not be accessed (the domain is blocked for this research tool), so firsthand forum evidence is thin. The only concrete overposting anecdote found is a vendor's own new Page: about 25–30 Reels/day led to views near zero within a week. Clip/meme-page lore of "5–10+ Reels/day" could **not** be verified from accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
- [ANECDOTE, vendor blog] A Reels-uploader tool's blog reports that its own **new Page posting 25–30 Reels/day saw views fall to near zero within a week**. The same source says staying under the 30/day API cap "does not guarantee safety" and recommends a **much lower cap on new Pages**. Meta publishes no limit for manual in-app posting. This comes from search-result snippets only; the page returned 404 when fetched. Treat it as a single, unverified account. — [Reel Uploader: Daily Posting Limits for New Accounts (2026)](https://reeluploader.com/blog/daily-limits-new-accounts)
- [BLOG] A 2025 creator blog on Facebook Content Monetization warns that spammy posts and engagement bait "will get flagged fast", including memes. It suggests adding real changes (re-editing, rewording, remixing), and reports that static memes turned into Reels often outperform the original. — [Mark Morphew: How to get approved by Facebook's Content Monetization program (2025)](https://www.markmorphew.com/how-to-get-approved-by-facebooks-content-monetization-bonus-program/)
- [BLOG] Searches on Facebook monetization for clip and meme pages mostly turned up **paid courses with unverified earnings claims** (e.g., Gumroad "first $100 in 7 days" products). These are not reliable evidence on posting volume. — e.g., [Gumroad example](https://albinaparker.gumroad.com/l/facebook-page-monetization-first-100-in-7-days)

### Inferences
- Without verifiable forum data, the safest reading is this: very high volume (25–30/day) on a **new** Page is a known reported failure pattern. Moderate volume (a few per day) is what the general guidance supports. The middle ground (5–10/day on an established Page) is unproven either way.
- For clip pages, what limits reach is more likely the **originality and duplication signals** (section 5) than raw count. The anecdotal reach collapses may be the spam classifier reacting to bursts of similar content, not a "too many posts" rule.

### Gaps
- No accessible Reddit (r/Facebook, r/socialmedia, r/content_marketing), Facebook-group or YouTube creator testimony with concrete numbers was retrieved. The reach-drop threshold for gaming clip pages is unknown.
- No Brazilian forum or creator report (mLabs, Etus, RD Station, Rock Content or creator videos) with Facebook Reels/day numbers was found.

## 4. Hard limits: Facebook Reels Publishing API and other caps

### Takeaway
The only hard, official number: **30 API-published Reels per Page per rolling 24 hours** on `POST /{page_id}/video_reels`. It is a technical ceiling, not a recommendation, and staying under it does not protect against spam or reach penalties.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "Reels API is limited to 30 API-published posts within a 24-hour moving period." The limit "is enforced on the `POST /{page_id}/video_reels` endpoint when attempting to publish a reel", and Meta recommends that apps enforce it themselves, especially for scheduled posts. Other limits in the same doc:
  - 10 collaborator invitations per Page per 24 h
  - reels must be 3–90 s (60 s max as a Page story)
  - 9:16 recommended at 1080×1920 (minimum 540×960), 24–60 fps
  - a scheduled publish time must be more than 10 minutes ahead and within 29 days
  - files hosted on sites that block access via robots.txt, or on Meta's CDN, are rejected
  - Pages only (no profiles)
  
  — [Meta for Developers: Reels Publishing API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/video-api/guides/reels-publishing)
- [VENDOR] Metricool's help center lists Facebook's API limit as "30 Reels posts" per rolling 24 hours. It notes that limits are set by the networks and may change. — [Metricool: Daily Posting Limits on Social Media](https://help.metricool.com/en/article/daily-posting-limits-on-social-media-kcqb91)
- [VENDOR] A developer blog says the window is rolling (the last 24 h, not calendar days) and that Meta documents **no appeal or way to raise the limit**. This comes from a search snippet. — [singhamandeep.com: Facebook Reels API](https://singhamandeep.com/facebook-reels-api/)
- [VENDOR] Blotato lists a separate **25 posts per 24 h per Facebook Page** general post limit for its API product. This is not confirmed in Meta docs and comes from a search summary only. — [Blotato Facebook API](https://www.blotato.com/api/facebook)

### Inferences
- If the tool posts 10–20 clips/day across several Pages, each Page stays well under 30/24 h. The scheduler should still enforce its own per-Page cap far below 30 (Meta explicitly asks apps to enforce the limit), count in a rolling 24 h window, and handle the rate-limit error gracefully.
- The ">10 min ahead, ≤29 days" scheduling rule matters if the tool uses Meta-side scheduling instead of its own queue.

### Gaps
- No official Meta limit for **manual** (in-app) Reels posting was found.
- No documented Graph API error code or behavior for exceeding the 30/24 h limit was captured.

## 5. Meta's 2025 "unoriginal content" crackdown (brief; covered in depth elsewhere)

### Takeaway
This matters for frequency in one specific way. Posting **many** clips of other creators' footage, or the **same** clip across several Pages, increases exposure to duplicate detection and the spam-network rules. Volume multiplies the risk; it does not create it.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Duplicate videos get reduced distribution. Accounts that repeatedly repost others' content without meaningful enhancement lose monetization for a period and get reduced reach. Commentary and "unique take" content is said to be exempt. — [Meta for Creators: Combating unoriginal content (Jul 2025)](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content/?locale=en_GB); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- [OFFICIAL] Networks of many accounts sharing the same content lose monetization eligibility and may lose reach. — [Meta Newsroom, Apr 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/cracking-down-spammy-content-facebook/)

### Inferences
- For a multi-Page setup, posting the **identical** rendered clip to several Pages fits the description of "networks… sharing the same content". It is safer to give each Page distinct clips, or clearly different editorial versions (narration or headline), rather than raising the clip count per Page. This is about editorial differentiation, not altering the video to avoid copyright detection, which the project rules forbid.

### Gaps
- Enforcement status and details as of Nov 2026 (any 2026 updates to the originality rules) were not verified.

## 6. New vs. established Pages

### Takeaway
No official or data-study source gives different numbers for new and established Pages. The only explicit advice to start **lower on new Pages** comes from a tool vendor, backed by its own anecdote of collapse at 25–30/day.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTE] This vendor recommends a much lower daily cap for new Pages than the 30/day API ceiling, and reports a new Page collapsing to near-zero views within a week at 25–30 Reels/day. — [Reel Uploader: Daily Posting Limits for New Accounts (2026)](https://reeluploader.com/blog/daily-limits-new-accounts)
- [DATA] Socialinsider's sample shows most brand posting volume comes from larger Pages: 13.5M of the 24.6M posts were from the 100K-follower band, against about 1.08M from Pages under 5K followers. That suggests bigger Pages post far more per Page, though the study does not tie this to reach outcomes. — [Socialinsider posting-frequency study](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-posting-frequency/)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta's only related guidance is to use the Professional Dashboard to see how content resonates, and to check the Page's recommendation and monetization status. — [Meta for Creators best practices (OLDER, 2022)](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/best-practices-for-growing-your-facebook-presence); [Combating unoriginal content (2025)](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content/?locale=en_GB)

### Inferences (proposed operating numbers, synthesized from the above; not from any single source)
- **New Page (first ~2–4 weeks):** about **1–3 Reels/day**, spaced several hours apart (for example morning, midday and evening Brazil time), and consistent every day. This matches the 1–2/day generic guidance and the vendor's "start low" warning.
- **Ramp-up:** if reach per Reel and the Page-status tools stay healthy, go up step by step (for example +1 Reel/day per week) to about **3–5 Reels/day**.
- **Established Page with stable reach:** **5–10 Reels/day** is plausible, but **unverified**. Keep it well below the 30/24 h API ceiling, never burst-post, and roll back if average reach per Reel falls for several days in a row.
- For a 10–20 clips/day production line, the volume is best **spread across several distinct Pages, each with distinct clips**, rather than concentrated on one Page or duplicated across Pages.

### Gaps
- No controlled data compares reach per Reel at different daily volumes for new vs. established Pages.
- Whether Facebook applies any "trust" or "warm-up" period to new Pages is not documented officially.
