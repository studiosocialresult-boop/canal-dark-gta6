# Originality / reused-content rules and risks for high-volume clip ("cortes") channels on YouTube Shorts, Instagram, Facebook and TikTok (state as of Nov 2026)

Legend for source type: **[OFFICIAL]** = platform's own policy/help page or official blog; **[PRESS]** = news/trade press reporting; **[BLOG]** = marketing/SEO/tool vendor blog (commercial interest); **[ANECDOTE]** = forum post, creator testimony, AI summary of a video. Dates flagged when older than 2025.

## 1. YouTube: "inauthentic content" (ex-"repetitious") + "reused content" + the Oct 2026 Shorts originality update — what counts as enough transformation, and does mass-uploading similar Shorts get flagged?

### Takeaway
YouTube has three overlapping layers: (1) YPP monetization policies ("reused content" and "inauthentic content", renamed 15 Jul 2025) that act on the whole channel; (2) a Shorts-specific rule that excludes reuploads/unedited clips/compilations from revenue sharing; and (3) since ~1 Oct 2026, a Shorts **recommendation** change that cuts reach for channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading" others' videos. There is no published per-day upload cap; the risk from volume comes from content looking templated/interchangeable and from the channel being classified as an aggregator, not from the raw number of uploads.

### Cited Findings
**Inauthentic content (YPP) — [OFFICIAL]**
- The 15 Jul 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and clarified that it covers content that is "repetitive or mass-produced". The policy body still uses the heading "Generic or Repetitive Content". — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Definition: content that "looks like it's made with a template", or that may feel repetitive after several videos from the same channel in a row; "channels where content feels interchangeable from video to video are not allowed to monetize." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different"; series where each video has "a distinct storyline, focus, or concept". Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie called the July 2025 change a "minor update" to long-standing YPP policy; press noted it does not ban mass-produced content from the platform, only from monetization. — [PRESS] [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)

**Reused content (YPP) — [OFFICIAL]**
- Reused content = repurposing content already online "without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value." Test: whether "viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video." — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Allowed examples: "Using clips for a critical review"; "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video"; "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary"; "Replays of a sports tournament where you explain the moves a competitor did to succeed". — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Not allowed: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative"; "Short videos you compiled from other social media websites"; "Content uploaded many times by other creators"; "Promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)". — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The policy applies "even if you have permission from the original creator" and is separate from copyright. "Our reused content policy applies to your channel as a whole" — "monetization may be removed from your entire channel." The page's definition of "video" explicitly includes Shorts. — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)

**Shorts monetization — [OFFICIAL]**
- Views not eligible for Shorts revenue sharing include "Non-original Shorts, such as unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows", "reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or other platform", and "compilations with no original content added". — [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)

**Spam policy (applies to all content, not just monetization) — [OFFICIAL]**
- "Scraped content": "Re-posting material from other websites or platforms, or other videos without adding anything of your own"; original commentary, educational value or unique editing can make a difference. — [YouTube spam, deceptive practices & scams policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- "Automated or synthetic mass-production": "we don't allow use of these tools to flood our platform with repetitive content"; posting a few variations of a video is allowed. The policy also covers "coordinated networks of channels." — [YouTube spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- Consequences: monetization suspension or channel/account termination; for some violations removal + warning or strike; "If you get 3 strikes within 90 days, your channel may be terminated." — [YouTube spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)

**Oct 2026 Shorts recommendation change — [PRESS citing an OFFICIAL TeamYouTube Help Community post]**
- TeamYouTube post "Prioritizing original content on Shorts" (~1 Oct 2026): "we're updating our recommendation systems to further prioritize original content" and "reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own." — [Dexerto, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/); [Social Media Today, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-content/)
- "Channels identified as primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes will likely see less distribution" — i.e., judged at channel level; no ratio, lookback window, notification, or status indicator published. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)
- Guidance: avoid "minor technical edits or template-based changes simply to alter a video"; "If you use clips from another creator, make sure you're adding real value through your own original commentary, unique edits, or storytelling." — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-content/)
- PPC Land paraphrases Rene Ritchie as saying that **voice-over narration that merely describes on-screen events**, minor technical edits, and template-based bulk changes do not make a clip original, and suggesting the Posts tab for sharing others' videos. Dexerto and Social Media Today do not mention Ritchie; this is a single-source paraphrase, not a verified quote. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/); not confirmed by [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/)
- Dexerto frames the change as a crackdown on "clipping channels" (it mentions clippers paid by streamers such as N3on and Clavicular); 9to5Google noted YouTube has not defined how much original input is enough. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-clipping-channels-in-shorts-update-prioritizing-original-content-3415700/)

**Enforcement against mass production — [PRESS]**
- Jan 2026: YouTube removed channels reported as 16 channels / ~35M subscribers / 4.7B views (another report: 11 terminated, 6 wiped); a YouTube spokesperson said removals were under spam and deceptive-practices policies, not a new anti-AI rule. Counts conflict between outlets. — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589980/youtube-cracks-down-deletes-47b-views-of-ai-spam-videos); summarized via search results, figures not independently verified.
- Reports of false-positive terminations (e.g., an animation channel with 650K subs terminated for "spam and scam" on 13 Nov 2025). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-ceo-defends-ai-moderation-as-creators-lose-channels-overnight/) (via search summary)

**Upload volume itself**
- No official daily upload limit for Shorts was found. A Benzinga headline says a YouTube Shorts chief debunked the myth that posting 10 videos a day hurts performance — [PRESS, headline only, page returned 403; date/speaker not verified, likely 2023 = older](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532)

### Inferences
- For a GTA 6 cortes operation, the two big YouTube risks are (a) losing **channel-wide** monetization under reused/inauthentic content, and (b) being classed as an "aggregator" in the Shorts feed after Oct 2026. Both are pattern-based (the channel as a whole), so raising volume with lightly-edited clips amplifies the pattern; raising volume with genuinely commented clips does not, per the policy text.
- The user's "narrado" mode (full PT-BR narration with analysis/opinion/context) fits the allowed examples ("add a storyline and commentary", "explain the moves"). The "original" mode (original audio + at most one narrated hook + subtitles/headline/template) is closer to the not-allowed examples ("clips … edited together with little or no narrative") and to what TeamYouTube called "template-based changes". A narration that only describes what is on screen may not count (single-source Ritchie paraphrase), so narration should add information/opinion (news context, release dates, comparisons), not just describe.
- A fixed visual template is fine as "same intro/outro" packaging, but if the template + translation is the *main* change across 10–20 daily uploads, the channel risks reading as "made with a template / interchangeable".

### Gaps
- No official threshold for "primarily aggregating" (share of uploads, lookback window) — YouTube has not published one.
- Could not open the TeamYouTube Help Community post directly; exact wording relies on press quotes.
- No official statement on whether translating + subtitling foreign-language footage counts as "significant modification" (none found).
- Benzinga "10 a day doesn't hurt" article could not be read (403).

## 2. Facebook (Meta): July 2025 unoriginal-content crackdown — penalties and what counts as "meaningful enhancement"

### Takeaway
Since 14 Jul 2025 Facebook cuts distribution of **all** content and removes monetization for accounts that repeatedly repost others' content without meaningful enhancement, and demotes duplicate copies in favour of the original. Voice-over, commentary and creative edits are named as enough; stitching clips or adding a watermark are named as not enough.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Announced 14 Jul 2025. Accounts that repeatedly reuse others' videos/photos/text without credit "perderão o acesso aos programas de monetização do Facebook" and "terão a distribuição de todo o conteúdo que compartilham reduzida". — [Facebook Creators blog: Combating unoriginal content](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- [OFFICIAL] Meaningful enhancement examples: "edições criativas, sobreposição de voz ou comentários" (creative edits, voice-over, commentary); reactions with commentary and joining trends are welcome. Not enough: "Não basta apenas juntar clipes ou adicionar sua marca d'água." — [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- [OFFICIAL] Duplicates: when systems detect duplicate videos, Meta will reduce distribution of the copies so the original gets the views, and is testing links on duplicates pointing to the original. — [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- [OFFICIAL] Best practices: post original content (Pages get more distribution when they mainly post content they filmed/created); tell a story; avoid "vídeos muito curtos que ofereçam pouco valor"; avoid visible third-party watermarks and visibly recycled content from other apps; captions without links, minimal caps, up to five hashtags. — [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- [OFFICIAL] Post-level insights in the Professional Dashboard and the Support home screen show whether a Page is at risk of penalties to monetization or recommendations. Rollout "gradual over the coming months". — [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-introduces-stronger-measures-against-unoriginal-content-creators/)
- [PRESS] Monetization programs affected reportedly include Performance Bonus, in-stream ads and Ads on Reels; Meta said it acted against ~500K accounts for spammy behaviour and removed ~10M profiles impersonating large creators in H1 2025. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/facebook-push-against-unoriginal-content/); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/after-youtube-meta-crackdowns-on-unoriginal-content-removes-10-million-fake-facebook-profiles-74532.htm); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/)

### Inferences
- Because Facebook penalizes the **account's whole distribution**, a Page posting 10–20 lightly edited GTA clips/day is exposed to an account-level demotion that also hurts its good posts.
- The "reduce distribution of duplicate copies" rule implies that the same clip on several Facebook Pages owned by the operator may have all but one copy demoted (no official statement on same-owner duplicates was found).
- Facebook is the platform that most explicitly lists **voice-over** as qualifying enhancement — favourable to the "narrado" mode.

### Gaps
- No official numeric threshold for "repeatedly".
- No official statement on whether translating/subtitling counts as enhancement.
- No data on how long the distribution penalty lasts.

## 3. Instagram: originality ranking 2022 → 2026 (aggregators removed from recommendations; watermarks)

### Takeaway
Instagram has escalated in three steps (2022 ranking boost for originals; Apr 2024 original replaces repost in recommendations and repeat aggregators become non-recommendable; Apr–May 2026 extension to photos/carousels with a rolling 30-day check). Penalty is loss of reach to non-followers, not removal. Commentary, voiceover, unique text and creative edits count; credit, watermarks, crops, borders, speed changes do not.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS, 2022 = older] Mosseri announced in Apr 2022 that ranking would value original content more and admitted detecting originality is hard. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagram-ranking-algorithm-original-content-200028046.html); [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/instagram-ranking-system-recommend-original-content/)
- [PRESS, 30 Apr 2024] If Instagram finds identical content it recommends only the original (Explore, Reels, in-feed recommendations); reposts get a label linking to the original; accounts that repeatedly repost content they didn't create or enhance are removed from recommendations but still reach followers; content "significantly changed" (e.g., meme or parody) is not replaced. TechCrunch gives **no** numeric threshold. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators)
- [PRESS, 30 Apr 2026] Policy extended from Reels to photos and carousels: "accounts that repost too many photos and carousels will become ineligible for algorithmic recommendations"; Instagram's blog lists "unique text, creative edits, and voiceover on a photo or video" and says the best creators "take third-party content and make it unmistakably theirs"; changing speed or adding a screenshot of the original poster's name is not enough. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- [BLOG, updated 5 May 2026] Mosseri: "If most of what you post to Instagram is someone else's content, your account is no longer going to be recommendable." Rolling 30-day window; not enough = watermark, slight crop, crediting the creator in caption; enough = commentary, remixing, reinterpretation, original framing. Near-identical reposts are replaced by the original, whose creator is notified. — [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy)
- [PRESS/BLOG, conflicting] One report says Instagram monitored Reels in May 2026 and photos/carousels in June; another says live by 2 May. A secondary claim that Reels from accounts posting unoriginal content "10 or more times over the previous 30 days" stop being recommended is **not supported** by the TechCrunch 2024 article. Another secondary report says reuploading with a different caption, border, watermark **or subtitles** is flagged as unoriginal. — search summaries of [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy), [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/instagrams-aggregator-purge-original-creators-gain-ground-as-repost-empires-crumble/); contradicted on the "10 times" figure by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators)
- [PRESS] Mosseri later said original content reach increased significantly after the April 2026 change (no figures given). — [Social Media Today, Oct 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-updates-shorts-algorithm-to-put-more-focus-on-original-content/)
- [PRESS, ~2024] Mosseri: your **own** logo on a Reel does not hurt reach; Instagram tries not to recommend Reels carrying other apps' logos/watermarks (e.g., TikTok, CapCut). — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)
- [PRESS, 2021 = older] Instagram made content "visibly recycled from other apps" less discoverable in the Reels tab; followers still see it. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-now-limit-the-reach-of-re-posts-from-tiktok-within-its-reels/594803)

### Inferences
- The 2026 rule is a **share-of-posts** test ("most of what you post"), so posting more unedited clips raises the unoriginal share; posting more clips with real commentary does not.
- PT-BR subtitles + headline + template alone likely fall on the "not enough" side for Instagram (subtitles/borders/captions named as insufficient in secondary reporting); narration with opinion/context likely qualifies ("voiceover", "commentary").
- Cross-posting the user's own export is fine as long as the file has no other app's watermark (export clean masters from the tool).

### Gaps
- No primary Instagram/Meta page with the exact 2026 criteria was retrieved (Instagram blog quoted only via Tubefilter/PetaPixel/Planoly).
- No official numeric threshold; "most" is the only official-sounding wording.
- How to check account status / appeals for this specific rule not confirmed from a primary source.

## 4. TikTok: unoriginal / low-quality content and For You feed (FYF) eligibility

### Takeaway
TikTok's official rule is short: content with "unoriginal or reused material without anything new" is ineligible for the FYF (not removed), and repeated FYF violations can make the **whole account** temporarily ineligible. Unoriginal content is also excluded from Creator Rewards. Multiple accounts are allowed, but not to deceive or evade bans.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Community Guidelines (page shows effective 24 Sep 2026, released 25 Aug 2026): "You should only post content you created or have the right to share." "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new." — [TikTok CG: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] Multiple accounts: "You can have multiple accounts—for example, for fan content or creative expression—but not to deceive others"; TikTok may "Ban additional or new accounts you create"; "If your account is restricted or banned, you may not create or use another account to get around it." Spam and inauthentic metrics are prohibited. — [TikTok CG: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] "We don't allow content that violates someone else's intellectual property (IP) rights … we will remove it." — [TikTok CG: Integrity & Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL] FYF ineligible content stays on the platform; creators can see in analytics if a video was made ineligible for recommendation; low views don't necessarily mean a rule was broken. — [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- [OFFICIAL, Apr 2024 = older but still relevant] TikTok added a policy that can make an entire account temporarily ineligible for the FYF if a creator repeatedly posts FYF-ineligible content. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/cgupdate?lang=en-ZA)
- [BLOG, citing TikTok Creator Academy] Unoriginal content = material largely repurposed from another source without creative edits, or combined from multiple sources with little or no added information/value; examples named include clips with someone else's watermark/logo and minimally edited content. Unoriginal content is ineligible for the Creator Rewards Program, which uses originality in its formula. — search summaries of [TikTok Creator Academy originality policy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/tiktok-originality-policy) (page did not render when fetched) and [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok)

### Inferences
- The account-level FYF ineligibility means that at 10–20 posts/day, a run of FYF-ineligible clips can throttle the whole account temporarily, including the good clips.
- TikTok does not forbid multiple accounts per se; running several accounts with the same clips is a grey zone that becomes a violation if it is deceptive (e.g., posing as independent accounts, inflating reach, evading a ban).

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the Creator Academy originality article text (page rendered empty); its examples are second-hand.
- No official TikTok statement found on posting the same video to several accounts, or on posting-frequency limits.
- Repeat-infringer (IP) account consequences not shown on the fetched page.

## 5. Cross-posting the same clip to TikTok/Reels/Shorts/Facebook, and running multiple accounts with the same clips

### Takeaway
Posting your **own** clean master to different platforms is not penalized by any rule found; what is penalized is a visible other-app watermark (Instagram, Facebook) and content recognized as a duplicate of someone else's. Posting the same clip to **several accounts on the same platform** is where risk concentrates: Facebook/Instagram demote duplicates to one "original", YouTube's spam policy targets coordinated channel networks, TikTok allows multiple accounts only if not deceptive, and penalties/terminations can spill over to linked channels.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS] Instagram: own logo OK; other apps' logos (TikTok, CapCut) reduce recommendation. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)
- [OFFICIAL] Facebook: avoid visible third-party watermarks and content visibly recycled from other apps; duplicate copies get reduced distribution in favour of the original. — [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube reused content lists "Content uploaded many times by other creators" as not allowed; spam policy covers "coordinated networks of channels". — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [YouTube spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube copyright: a terminated channel's owner "can't create new channels." — [YouTube copyright strike basics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok: multiple accounts allowed "but not to deceive others"; additional accounts can be banned; no ban evasion. — [TikTok CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [OFFICIAL, comparison only] X explicitly prohibits operating multiple accounts that post substantially similar or identical content (localized language versions allowed). — [X platform manipulation policy](https://help.x.com/rules-and-policies/platform-manipulation)
- [PRESS, date not shown — likely 2022 = older] Brazil: YouTube penalties on Flow Podcast also hit six other channels linked to the business (monetization and reach). — [Gazeta do Povo](https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/vida-e-cidadania/flow-podcast-youtube-rigidas-penalizacoes/)
- [BLOG] A 2026 guide claims there is no algorithmic penalty for posting the same video to TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, X, LinkedIn — vendor claim, not a platform statement. — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/avoid-content-duplication-penalties-cross-posting-2026)

### Inferences
- Safe pattern: one clean, watermark-free master per clip, posted once per platform (one main account per platform), with platform-native captions/hashtags.
- Risky pattern: same clip on N accounts on the same platform. Expected effects: duplicate demotion (Meta), "Content uploaded many times" / network signals (YouTube), possible "deceptive multiple accounts" (TikTok), and linked-account spillover if one gets penalized. If several accounts exist, each should have a distinct editorial angle/content, not mirrored uploads.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube/Meta/TikTok text found that directly says "same owner, same video on two of your own accounts" is or isn't allowed.
- No data on whether cross-platform duplicates (same clip on TikTok and Reels) are detected and treated as unoriginal when the poster is the same person on both.

## 6. What volume do Brazilian "canais de cortes" and gaming/news Shorts channels actually use? (and what data says about frequency vs reach)

### Takeaway
No reliable journalistic source gives "X cortes/day" for Brazilian cortes channels or GTA/gaming news Shorts channels. Available evidence is anecdotal or vendor data: Brazilian creators report 2–3/day and one case cut from 8 to 3/day to grow; the largest frequency datasets (Buffer) show more posts per week raise the *chance* of a hit, while the median post stays flat. The clipping economy in 2026 scales by many **accounts/clippers**, not by one channel posting dozens per day, and YouTube's Oct 2026 change targets exactly that model.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL/PRESS, Brazil] Podpah CEO Victor Assis: the show is a "tiro de canhão" with the lives and also "long tail" through cortes released "entre duas ou três horas depois", making content last months. — [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/victor-assis-ceo-do-podpah)
- [ANECDOTE/BLOG, Brazil] A cortes creator who produced as many videos/day as possible cut from 8 to 3 per day after advice that a new channel confuses YouTube about whom to distribute to; growth improved. — [Tactus](https://tactus.com.br/?p=36277) (via search summary)
- [ANECDOTE, AI summary of a video, Brazil, Dec 2025] A cortes Shorts channel monetized in 90 days posting 2–3 videos daily while editing 4–5 per day to keep a buffer. — [Lilys AI note](https://lilys.ai/notes/pt/youtube-shorts-20251211/monetize-youtube-shorts-90-days)
- [BLOG, vendor] AIR Media-Tech recommends starting at 1 Short/day and increasing gradually; claims that in 2025 posting 10+ Shorts/day doubled views for its "experts" (unverified). — [AIR Media-Tech (PT)](https://air.io/pt/youtube-hacks/how-often-should-you-post-shorts-to-grow-faster)
- [BLOG, vendor] Other guides conflict (one says >2 Shorts/day on the same channel shows diminishing returns/cannibalization; another says no algorithmic reason to prefer 5 vs 3 if quality is equal). — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-how-many-per-day-2026) (via search summary; unverified)
- [VENDOR DATA, Oct 2025] Buffer, 11M+ TikTok posts from 150K+ accounts: vs 1 post/week, 2–5/week ≈ +17% views per post, 6–10/week ≈ +29%, 11+/week ≈ +34%; median views per post stay roughly flat (~500) — gains come from more shots at a viral hit. — [Buffer TikTok frequency study](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/)
- [VENDOR DATA, Aug 2025] Buffer, 2.1M Instagram posts from 102K accounts: 3–5 posts/week ≈ +12% reach per post vs baseline; other Buffer page cites ≈ +18% for 6–9/week and ≈ +24% for 10+/week (figures differ across Buffer pages). — [Buffer Instagram frequency study](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- [PRESS, May 2026] NPR: "clipping economy" — thousands of clippers flood platforms with clips of podcasts, sports, films; paid per view. — [NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet)

### Inferences
- The defensible single-channel volume implied by the evidence is low single digits per day per account (2–5), scaled up only if each clip stands on its own (distinct topic + real commentary) and analytics show no drop in per-clip views/retention. 10–20/day on one account is far above any documented successful Brazilian cortes practice found here.
- For a GTA 6 news niche, the number of genuinely new news items per day is the natural cap: posting more clips than there are distinct stories pushes toward "interchangeable/templated" content (YouTube inauthentic) and toward aggregator classification.
- A 10–20/day production is better spread across platforms (e.g., a few per day per platform) than concentrated on one account or mirrored across several accounts on one platform.

## 7. Copyright: Content ID / takedowns on GTA 6 trailers and other creators' footage, and how high volume accumulates risk (rules only)

### Takeaway
On YouTube, Content ID claims usually don't cause strikes, but manual takedown requests do; 3 active copyright strikes within 90 days can terminate the channel and bar new channels. Take-Two/Rockstar has a documented record of claiming/striking GTA 6 material on YouTube (full trailer in reaction videos, leaks, mods). At 10–20 clips/day that reuse trailer or other creators' footage, the number of claimable/strikeable videos grows linearly, so a single rightsholder sweep can produce several strikes quickly.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "Channels that get 3 copyright strikes in 90 days are subject to termination"; terminated channels lose uploads and the owner "can't create new channels". Strikes expire in 90 days if Copyright School is completed and there are fewer than 3. — [YouTube copyright strike basics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] "If you get a Content ID claim on your video, it typically doesn't result in a copyright strike"; a claim can lead to a strike if disputed without valid reason and the owner then files a valid removal request. "Videos can only have 1 copyright strike at a time" (each video is a separate strike exposure). Live stream removed for copyright → live restricted 7 days (14 after a second). — [YouTube copyright strike basics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's reused-content policy is separate from copyright and applies even with permission. — [YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok removes IP-infringing content. — [TikTok CG](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- [PRESS] Take-Two claimed a reaction video that played the full GTA 6 trailer with audio (creator TheProfessional objected). — [SVG](https://svg.com/1463343/gta-6-trailer-takedown-controversy)
- [PRESS] Take-Two removed leaked GTA 6 footage from YouTube and Twitter; struck a GTA 5 map-mod video built from GTA 6 trailer/leak data (modder pulled all work after a single strike). — [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/take-two-strikes-gta-6-leaked-footage-seemingly-confirming-legitimacy); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/grand-theft-auto/weeks-after-modding-gta-6s-map-to-gta-5-modder-pre-emptively-pulls-all-their-work-offline-after-a-single-youtube-strike-from-rockstars-owner)
- [PRESS] Reports of fan-made/AI fake GTA 6 trailers vanishing from YouTube after Take-Two action; a 2025 report alleged abuse of the copyright system by an individual flagging GTA 6 trailer videos. — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/GTA-6-trailer-3-release-anticipated-as-Take-Two-targets-fake-AI-generated-leaks.1351256.0.html); [GamingHQ](https://gaminghq.eu/2025/05/07/ayeyonino-accused-of-abusing-copyright-system-to-flag-gta-6-trailer-videos)

### Inferences
- Legitimate ways to stay within the rules: use short excerpts of official trailers only as support for real commentary/news (fair-use-style transformative use is the platforms' "commentary/review" category), avoid reposting full trailers or long uninterrupted segments, avoid leaked footage (Take-Two actively strikes it), prefer creators' content only with permission and with substantive commentary, and keep claimed clips counted so the operation stops reusing a source that triggers claims. Track claims/strikes per channel in the database and pause uploads from a source when one arrives.
- Because strikes are per video and termination is per channel (and blocks new channels), concentrating 10–20 daily trailer-heavy clips on one YouTube channel is the highest-risk configuration.

### Gaps
- Meta Rights Manager and TikTok repeat-infringer thresholds were not retrieved (no primary source read).
- No official Take-Two/Rockstar policy on fan/news use of trailer footage was found; evidence is case-by-case press reports (several from 2023–2025).
